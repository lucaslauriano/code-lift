A country has `N` cities numbered `0` to `N - 1`, connected by `N - 1` two-way roads that form a
tree. The roads are given as an array `T`, where `T[i]` is the city on the other end of the road
from city `i`.

Exactly one city is the capital, and it is marked by `T[i] === i` — a road from a city to itself,
which is not a real road.

Return an array where position `k` holds the number of cities at distance `k + 1` from the capital.
The array runs up to the largest distance that actually occurs; the capital itself is at distance
`0` and is never counted.

## Example

Given `T = [9, 1, 4, 9, 0, 4, 8, 9, 0, 1]`:

`T[1] === 1`, so city `1` is the capital. The answer is `[1, 3, 2, 3]` — one city is one road away,
three cities are two roads away, two are three away and three are four away. That accounts for all
nine cities other than the capital.

For `T = [0]` the country is a single city, which is the capital, so the answer is `[]`.

## Assumptions

- `N` is an integer within the range `[1 .. 100,000]`.
- Each element of `T` is an integer within the range `[0 .. N - 1]`.
- Exactly one city satisfies `T[i] === i`, and the roads form a tree — every city is reachable.
- Expected worst-case time complexity is `O(N)`.
